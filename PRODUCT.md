# Product brief

## Purpose

Musica Mathematica is a browser-local teaching workbench for mathematical
representations of music. It supports inquiry with deterministic models,
bounded audio observations, controlled comparisons, and explicit limits on
interpretation.

It does not provide grading, diagnosis, calibrated measurement, complete
transcription, course administration, or remote collaboration.

## Users

The intended users are university and conservatoire music students and
instructors working in theory, analysis, acoustics, composition, ensemble, or
performance-technology settings.

## Primary workflow

Each lesson asks the learner to:

1. identify the representation and its assumptions;
2. record a prediction;
3. change a controlled factor;
4. compare trial results;
5. explain the mechanism and uncertainty;
6. test the idea through listening, performance, or score study; and
7. state what additional evidence would be needed.

## Product constraints

- Mathematical output must retain units, method, provenance, and calibration
  status.
- Model output must not be presented as an observation of performers, rooms, or
  networks.
- Audio-derived labels must remain observations or hypotheses.
- Portfolio data must stay browser-local unless the learner exports it.
- Raw audio, file names, media streams, and device identifiers must not enter
  portfolio storage.
- The interface must not imply grades, mastery, diagnosis, or validated
  learning outcomes.

## Interface requirements

- Keep the current inquiry stage and next action visible.
- Keep evidence type and interpretation limits adjacent to results.
- Use text or symbols in addition to color for status.
- Preserve keyboard focus visibility and reduced-motion behavior.
- Keep projection layouts readable without hiding privacy or calibration
  status.

These requirements guide implementation. The project has not established formal
WCAG conformance or classroom effectiveness.
